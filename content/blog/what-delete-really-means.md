**Short answer:** "Delete" means something different on every network. On Messenger it removes your copy only. On Instagram, unsending removes it from both sides. On Reddit it detaches your name but leaves copies elsewhere. On Facebook it goes to a trash for 30 days. Knowing which one you're getting decides whether the cleanup you're about to do is worth doing.

Here is what the button actually does, per platform.

## Messenger: your copy, not theirs

Deleting a conversation removes it from **your** inbox. The other person keeps the entire thread. Nothing about your deletion touches their device or their account.

*Delete for everyone* (unsend) is the exception — it removes one message you sent from everyone's view, if you use it in time.

## Instagram DMs: unsend is genuinely two-sided

Unsending removes the message from both sides of the conversation. It only works on messages you sent, and it doesn't touch anything they sent you.

This is the strongest "delete" on this list — closest to the thing people imagine when they press delete anywhere else.

## Reddit: attribution, not existence

Deleting removes the comment from Reddit and detaches your username. Copies taken before you deleted are unaffected, and deleting your account leaves everything you posted in place, attributed to `[deleted]`.

If the text matters more than the attribution, edit it before deleting — and understand that overwriting only affects copies made afterwards.

## X: gone from X, reachable in the archive

Deleting a post removes it from X. Your downloaded archive still contains it, because the archive is a snapshot from when you requested it. Quote-posts of a deleted post lose their content; screenshots obviously don't.

## Facebook posts: trash, then gone

Deleted posts and photos sit in Trash for about 30 days before Facebook removes them. Hiding from timeline is not deletion at all — the post remains reachable by direct link.

## The rule underneath all of it

> You control your copy and your attribution. You do not control copies other people or other systems already have.

Every difference above is a variation on that. Once you have it, deciding what to clean gets much simpler: if the risk you're managing is other people's copies, deletion is not the lever — and no tool changes that.

## What each cleanup is actually good for

- **Messenger / Facebook** — your own inbox and timeline, your own peace of mind.
- **Instagram unsend** — genuinely retracting something you sent.
- **Reddit / X** — breaking the link between your name and years of opinions, which is usually the real goal.
