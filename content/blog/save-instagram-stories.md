Stories vanish after 24 hours — that's the whole point of them, and exactly why you sometimes need one saved *now*: a friend's announcement, a recipe someone posted once, a memory you want to keep. Instagram only lets you save **your own** stories. For everything else, here are the real options, compared honestly.

## Method 1: Screen recording

The brute-force method: play the story, record your screen (Windows: **Win+Alt+R**; Mac: **Shift+Cmd+5**; phones have it in quick settings).

- **Pros:** works on anything, needs nothing.
- **Cons:** quality loss, UI elements baked into the recording, audio sync issues, photos become awkward video clips, and you must catch it live before it expires. Fine as a last resort; annoying as a habit.

## Method 2: Chrome DevTools

Your browser already downloaded the story media to show it to you — DevTools lets you keep it. Open the story, press **F12** → **Network** tab, filter **Img** for photos or **Media** for videos, and open the largest `cdninstagram.com` request in a new tab, then save it. Full quality, no tools installed.

- **Pros:** free, original quality, completely private.
- **Cons:** fiddly, one story frame at a time, and slow enough that a 12-frame story becomes a project. (We wrote a full [DevTools walkthrough](/blog/download-instagram-media-devtools) if you want this method.)

## Method 3: Story-downloader websites

Sites where you type a username and view their stories anonymously.

- **Pros:** no install.
- **Cons:** ad-infested, frequently broken, often compress the media — and the dangerous ones ask you to **log in with your Instagram account**. Never do that; those credentials get harvested. (More on that in [why you should never log in to downloader sites](/blog/never-login-to-instagram-downloader-sites).) Only usable for *public* accounts, and quality is a lottery.

## Method 4: A browser extension

An extension runs inside your own Instagram tab, so it sees exactly what you see and can save it directly — no separate site, no credentials shared, original quality.

## Which method should you use?

- **Once in a blue moon:** DevTools — free and clean, worth the fiddling.
- **Screen recording:** only when nothing else works.
- **Downloader websites:** skip them, especially any that asks you to log in.
- **Regularly:** the extension. One click, full quality, no risk.

Two honest notes to close: the person won't be notified when you save their story (screenshot notifications aren't a thing for stories in feeds) — but saving someone's content doesn't make it yours. Keep downloads for personal use, and ask before reposting.
