**Short answer:** The Activity Log is the only place Facebook lists everything you have ever posted, liked, commented on or been tagged in. Deleting from it sends most things to a trash that empties itself after about 30 days — so for a month, "deleted" is recoverable, by you and by Facebook.

That 30-day window is the part worth knowing before you start, because it is both a safety net and the reason nothing is instant.

## Where it is, and why it is the right screen

Profile → the three dots → **Activity log**. It is chronological, filterable by type and year, and it is where things you forgot about live: comments on pages that no longer exist, likes from 2013, photos other people tagged you in.

Everything else — scrolling your own timeline, opening each post — is a worse view of the same data.

## Delete, hide and untag are three different outcomes

The menu offers different options depending on the item, and they are not interchangeable:

- **Delete** — removes your own post or photo. Goes to trash first.
- **Hide from timeline** — the post still exists and is still reachable by direct link and by search. It is only removed from the timeline view.
- **Remove tag** — the other person's photo stays exactly where it is; your name comes off it.

> Hiding is the one people mistake for deleting. A hidden post has not been deleted by any definition.

For content someone else owns, untagging is the *only* option you control. If you need the photo itself gone, you have to ask them.

## The trash, and the 30 days

Deleted posts and photos go to Trash and are removed automatically after about 30 days. You can empty it manually to finish sooner.

Two implications:

1. **You can undo a mistake** for a month. Worth knowing before a big run.
2. **It is not gone when the log says it's gone.** If the point of the exercise is that something stops existing, empty the trash as a second step.

## Doing a large clear-out

The Activity Log has no select-all. Every item is a menu, an option and usually a confirmation — three clicks each, thousands of times, and the list re-renders as it shortens, which is what makes doing it by hand so error-prone.

Use the log's own filters to narrow to one type and one year at a time. Smaller batches are easier to check, and if you change your mind you have only affected a slice.
