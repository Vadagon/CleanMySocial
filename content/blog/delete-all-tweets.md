**Short answer:** You can delete your posts on X, but you can only reach the ones X will still show you. Its own timeline stops paging back after a few thousand posts, so an old account cannot be cleared through the interface alone. Request your archive first — it is the only complete list of what you posted.

This is the fact that decides whether any of this is worth starting, and almost no guide leads with it.

## The wall you will hit

X's profile timeline pages back a limited distance — in practice a few thousand posts — and then stops. Not a bug, not a tool limitation: it is what X exposes.

The consequences are worth stating plainly:

- If you posted 40,000 times since 2011, no browser tool can enumerate all of it, because X will not list it.
- Deleting from the top works fine until you reach that wall, and then the remaining history is simply unreachable.
- Filters help you *choose* within the reachable window; they do not extend it.

> "Delete all my tweets" realistically means "delete everything X still lists." For a heavy old account those are very different amounts.

## Get your archive first

Settings → *Your account* → *Download an archive of your data*. It takes a while to be prepared, and it gives you the complete record: every post, with dates and ids.

Do this even if you are about to delete everything. Two reasons:

1. It is the only copy you will have afterwards.
2. It tells you what you actually posted, which is often not what you remember posting.

## Deletion is one at a time, and paced

There is no bulk delete. Every removal is an individual action, and X rate-limits rapid sequences of them. Any tool that clears a large history is doing the same clicks you would, slowly enough not to get throttled — which means a long run, with the tab open.

Expect a cleanup measured in hours, not seconds, and expect it to pause. A tool that promises instant is either lying or about to get you rate-limited.

## Deleting posts is not the same as the other three things

Four separate cleanups, often confused:

- **Posts** — your own tweets.
- **Reposts** — undoing a repost removes your amplification, not the original.
- **Likes** — removing likes is separate again, and on a busy account there are far more of these than posts.
- **Follows** — unfollowing changes nothing about your own content.

Decide which you actually want. "Clean up my account" usually means posts and likes, and people forget likes are public.
