**Short answer:** Facebook has no setting that removes the News Feed. You have two real options: unfollow everyone, which empties the feed on Facebook's own terms but takes hours and gets refilled with suggestions — or hide the feed region in your browser, which is instant and reversible.

The reason you keep looking for that setting and not finding it is that the feed *is* the product. Everything else — messages, events, Marketplace, groups, the friend you actually wanted to look up — works fine without it.

## Option one: unfollow everyone

Unfollowing keeps the friendship and stops the posts. Do it to every friend, page and group and your feed goes quiet natively.

What it costs:

- It's per-person. Hundreds of clicks, and every new friend arrives followed.
- **It doesn't stay empty.** Facebook fills the space with suggested posts, Reels and groups you never followed.

> Unfollowing controls the part of the feed you chose. It does nothing to the part Facebook chose for you.

## Option two: hide the feed

The feed is one region of the page. Hide that region and the rest of Facebook is untouched — top bar, search, Messenger, notifications, Marketplace, every profile and group.

The honest description of what that does:

- **It is visual.** The posts are still delivered to your browser; you don't see them.
- **It does not reduce what Facebook collects.** You are still logged in, still loading the page.
- **It is instantly reversible.** Nothing is unfollowed, unfriended or changed on your account.

If your goal is "stop scrolling", visual is enough — a feed can't pull you in if it isn't rendered. If your goal is "Facebook knows less about me", neither hiding nor unfollowing is the tool; that's a question about what you log into at all.

## What about "Most recent"?

Switching to *Most recent* changes the ordering, not the existence. It is genuinely better than the ranked feed if you want a feed at all — chronological, nothing resurfaced from three days ago. But Facebook returns you to the default view regularly, so it behaves less like a setting and more like a preference you keep re-stating.

## The combination that works

1. Hide the feed, so the default screen is empty.
2. Hide stories and suggestions too — they are separate regions, and leaving them re-creates the scroll in a smaller box.
3. Leave everything else alone, so Facebook stays useful for whatever you open it for.
