## What happens when you give a website your Instagram password

When you type your credentials into a third-party site, you're not "logging in to Instagram." You're handing your password to whoever runs that server. From there, the common outcomes:

- **Credential harvesting.** Your login is stored and later used to hijack the account — for spam, follower-selling bots, or scamming your followers with "I'm in trouble, send money" messages.
- **Session theft.** Even sites that "only" ask you to log in to view private profiles are performing the login server-side and keeping the session token.
- **Credential stuffing.** If you reuse that password anywhere (email, banking…), those accounts are next.
- **Silent compromise.** The worst ones change nothing at first — they wait, while you have no idea anyone else has access.

Instagram's own security guidance is unambiguous: never enter your login on third-party sites, and revoke access from apps you don't trust.

## "But it needs my login to work"

No legitimate downloader needs your password. Public content is, by definition, viewable without logging in — a downloader site can fetch it the same way your browser does. The *only* reasons a site asks for credentials are (a) to steal them or (b) to do things with your account you wouldn't approve of. The "download private profiles" pitch is the biggest red flag of all — that's either a scam or a credential trap, full stop.

## Red flags checklist

- Asks for your Instagram username **and password**
- Promises access to **private** accounts
- Wants you to install a "helper app" or browser file from outside official stores
- Endless redirect chains and popup ads (each redirect is a monetization or malware opportunity)
- No identifiable company, no privacy policy

## If you already did it

1. Change your Instagram password immediately — and everywhere else you used it.
2. Turn on **two-factor authentication** (Settings → Accounts Center → Password and security).
3. Review **Where you're logged in** and log out unknown sessions.
4. Check connected apps and revoke anything unfamiliar.
