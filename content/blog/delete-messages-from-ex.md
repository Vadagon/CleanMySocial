There's a specific kind of bad moment: you're searching Messenger for an address someone sent you, and instead you scroll past three years of conversations with your ex. The relationship ended; the chat log didn't. If you're ready to actually clear it out — here's how to do a proper digital breakup cleanup, Messenger first.

## First, a quick decision: keep a copy or not?

Once you delete a conversation, your side of it is gone for good. If there's anything practical buried in there — receipts, addresses, photos you don't have elsewhere — download it first: **Settings → Your information → Download your information → Messages**. You can archive the file somewhere you'll never look and still have a clean inbox.

If you know there's nothing you need, skip this step. Cleaner that way.

## What deleting does (and doesn't do)

- Deleting the conversation removes it **from your account only**. Your ex keeps their copy — nothing you can do about that.
- **Unsend** ("Remove for everyone") works only on individual messages you sent, one at a time. It is not practical for erasing a whole relationship.
- Blocking does *not* delete history. A blocked person's conversation stays in your inbox until you delete it.

So the realistic goal is: your inbox, clean. Their inbox is their problem.

## Deleting the history

For one conversation, it's easy: hover the thread on `facebook.com/messages`, click ⋯ → **Delete chat** → confirm.

But it's rarely one conversation. There's the main thread, the group chats you were both in, the threads with their friends, their family, the shared-apartment logistics chat. If you're doing a full cleanup — or you've decided you want the *whole* inbox reset, not just one person surgically removed — that's hundreds of delete-and-confirm clicks, because Facebook has no bulk option.

(Frankly, not having to *look* at each thread while deleting it is half the value.)

## The rest of the checklist

Messenger is usually the biggest archive, but do a full sweep:

1. **Instagram** — DMs, tagged photos, close-friends list.
2. **Photos** — untag yourself on Facebook and Instagram; archive or delete shared albums.
3. **WhatsApp / iMessage** — delete the chats; export first if needed.
4. **Shared accounts** — Netflix, Spotify family, shared passwords. Change every password they ever knew.
5. **Relationship status and follows** — mute or unfollow if unfriending feels like too much; Facebook won't notify them either way.
6. **Google Photos / iCloud shared albums** — the one everyone forgets.

## One honest note

Delete because you're done, not to delete the fact that it happened — their copy exists regardless, and screenshots are forever. What you're cleaning is *your* space: your search results, your scroll history, your accidental 2 a.m. re-reads. That part is completely within your control, and it feels better than you'd expect.
