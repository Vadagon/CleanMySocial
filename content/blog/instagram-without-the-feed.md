**Short answer:** Instagram has a chronological *Following* feed that shows only accounts you follow, with no suggestions and no ads between posts. It works well and Instagram will not let you keep it — you get returned to the algorithmic Home feed constantly. That gap is the whole problem.

If you want Instagram for messages, for people you actually follow, or for a specific account you look up, most of what stops you is not the feed itself but what's stitched into it.

## The native option, and its one flaw

Tap the Instagram logo on Home and you can switch to **Following** — reverse-chronological, only accounts you follow. It is exactly what people ask for.

The flaw: it is not a setting. Close the app, come back later, and you are on Home again. You are choosing it every single time, which means in practice you use it about twice.

## What's actually in the Home feed

Four different things, and people conflate them:

1. Posts from accounts you follow.
2. **Suggested posts** from accounts you don't.
3. Reels, injected between posts.
4. Ads.

Only the first is what you signed up for. This matters when you decide what to hide: hiding "the feed" as one lump removes all four, but you may want the first back — in which case *Following* plus hidden suggestions is a better shape than hiding everything.

> The tiring part of Instagram is mostly the three things you didn't ask for.

## Stories and Explore are separate

Hiding the feed does nothing to the stories tray at the top, or to the grid on Explore, which is the most algorithmically aggressive surface on the app. Both are separate regions and both need their own decision.

An Instagram with the feed, stories and Explore suggestions hidden still has: DMs, search, your own profile, and any profile you navigate to directly. That is a usable messaging and lookup app with the slot machine removed.

## What hiding does not do

It doesn't reduce data collection, it doesn't stop the content being delivered to your browser, and it doesn't apply to the phone app — this is a browser extension, so it affects instagram.com. If most of your scrolling happens on your phone, be honest with yourself about whether fixing the desktop version changes anything.
