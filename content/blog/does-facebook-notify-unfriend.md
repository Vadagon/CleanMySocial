Short answer: **No. Facebook does not notify anyone when you unfriend them.** No alert, no email, no "X removed you" anywhere in their app. It's the question that stops most people from cleaning up their friends list, so let's settle it completely — including the indirect ways someone *could* notice.

## What actually happens when you unfriend someone

The moment you click Unfriend:

- You're removed from each other's friends lists (it's always mutual).
- They lose access to your **friends-only** posts and photos; they can still see anything you post as **Public**.
- Existing likes, comments, and Messenger history stay exactly where they are.
- **Nothing is sent to them.** Facebook has never had an unfriend notification, and third-party "who unfriended me" trackers violate Facebook's platform rules — the ones that "work" are surveys, scams, or credential traps. Don't install them, and don't fear them.

## The ways someone could still notice

No notification doesn't mean undetectable. Someone might notice if:

1. **They visit your profile** and see an "Add Friend" button where "Friends" used to be. This is the main tell — and it requires them to actually look.
2. **Your posts vanish from their feed.** Heavy posters are missed sooner; if you post rarely, your absence is invisible.
3. **Friend count arithmetic.** Someone would have to track your (or their) count and diff it. People who do this exist; they are rare.
4. **Mutual-friend surfaces** — you stop appearing in their "friends who like this" contexts.

Realistically: casual contacts essentially never notice, which is exactly why a [friends-list cleanup](/blog/facebook-friends-list-cleanup) is safer than it feels. The people most likely to notice are the ones checking your profile regularly — and if someone's doing that, ask yourself whether they belonged in your audience anyway.

## Related questions, quickly

- **Does unfollowing notify them?** No — and it's completely undetectable, since you stay friends. ([Full comparison here.](/blog/unfriend-vs-unfollow-vs-restrict-vs-block))
- **Does blocking notify them?** No notification, but it's the most discoverable: your profile becomes unfindable to them.
- **Can I re-add someone later?** Yes — they'd receive a normal friend request (that one *does* notify).
- **What if they send me a new request?** You can quietly hit Delete on it; that sends no notification either.

## The takeaway for your cleanup

The fear of "they'll know" is the main thing keeping people friends with 800 strangers. But the mechanics are on your side: no alert is sent, feeds are too noisy for absences to register, and the awkward-confrontation scenario almost never materializes.

The *actual* obstacle is effort — Facebook only lets you unfriend one person at a time, several clicks each.
