Accounts get hacked, disabled by mistake, or locked out of recovery every day — and for a lot of people, Instagram is where a decade of photos lives, nowhere else. If that's you, you need a backup. Here's how to get a *complete* one, including the things Instagram's official export quietly leaves out.

## Option 1: Instagram's official data export

Instagram will give you your data: **Settings → Accounts Center → Your information and permissions → Download your information**. Choose your account, select all data, pick **HTML or JSON**, and request the highest media quality. The archive arrives by email within a couple of days.

**What you get:** your posts, stories you posted, reels, messages, comments, likes, profile info.

**What's wrong with it:**

- **Media quality is often reduced** unless you explicitly select the higher-quality option — and even then, don't expect pristine originals for old uploads.
- The archive structure is built for data-protection compliance, not for humans: cryptic folder names, JSON metadata, files named like `409871234_n.jpg`.
- **It only covers *your* content.** And that's the big one, because…

## What the official export doesn't give you

- **Your Saved collections.** Years of carefully organized saved posts — recipes, references, inspiration — belong to *other people's accounts*, so they're not in your export. If those accounts delete their posts, your collection entries die with them.
- **Content from accounts you care about** — a partner's profile, a deceased relative's account, your business's old account you've lost access to.
- **A browsable structure.** An export you can't navigate is a backup you'll never use.

## The sensible backup routine

1. **Request the official export anyway.** It's the only source for your DMs, comments, and account metadata — keep it as the "legal" archive.
2. **Bulk-download your profile** for the actual full-quality media, in folders you can browse.
3. **Bulk-download your Saved collections** — the part no export will ever give you, and the part most likely to silently rot as others delete posts.
4. **Store it redundantly:** one copy on your computer, one in cloud storage or an external drive.
5. **Repeat every few months.** A backup from 2023 doesn't include 2026.

## Bottom line

The official export is a data dump; a real backup is something you can open and use. Do both — the export for completeness, a proper media download for the photos themselves — and your Instagram history stops depending on a login you could lose tomorrow.
