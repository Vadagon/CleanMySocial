**Short answer:** Facebook's supported unfriend flow works one profile at a time. For a small cleanup, use the built-in control. For a larger list, Mass Unfriender adds a selection step and performs the same removals in your signed-in browser.

At some point every long-time Facebook user looks at their friend count — 800, 1,500, more — and realizes they actually know maybe 150 of those people. And then discovers the catch: **Facebook has no way to unfriend more than one person at a time.** No checkboxes, no multi-select, nothing. Here's the manual method, and the bulk method.

## The manual way (official)

Facebook's supported flow is documented in the [Facebook Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/help/172936839431357):

1. Go to your friends list: [facebook.com/friends/list](https://www.facebook.com/friends/list).
2. Click the ⋯ menu next to a friend (or open their profile → **Friends** button).
3. Click **Unfriend**, confirm.
4. Find the next person. Repeat.

Each removal is several clicks, the list re-renders as you go, and Facebook's friends page only loads a chunk of your list at a time — so with hundreds of removals ahead of you, this is genuinely hours of work. Which is exactly why most people give up after twenty and keep carrying a decade of strangers.

## What unfriending actually does

Quick facts before you start cutting:

- **No notification is sent.** The person is not told you unfriended them; they'd only notice by visiting your profile. (Full details in [does Facebook notify on unfriend?](/blog/does-facebook-notify-unfriend))
- What they can still see depends on your audience and profile settings; Public content may remain visible.
- It's mutual — you also disappear from their friends list.
- You can re-add anyone later; they'd get a normal friend request.
- Unfriending is *not* blocking. They can still see your public profile and message you.

If someone just annoys you but cutting ties feels harsh, **Unfollow** (mute their posts, stay friends) might be enough — see our [unfriend vs. unfollow vs. restrict vs. block guide](/blog/unfriend-vs-unfollow-vs-restrict-vs-block).

## The bulk way: a browser extension

Since Facebook won't give you multi-select, a Chrome extension can add it — putting checkboxes directly on your friends list and automating the same unfriend clicks you'd do by hand, in your own logged-in browser.

## A sane way to decide who goes

Don't agonize per person; use rules. Remove anyone who is: a stranger you can't place, someone you haven't interacted with in 3+ years, an obviously dead or duplicate account, or a "friend" from a 2011 game you don't remember. Keep family, actual friends, and people you'd greet in the street. Everyone else is just an audience for your personal life — and [that has real privacy costs](/blog/privacy-risks-bloated-friends-list).

## Bottom line

Facebook's built-in flow removes one friend at a time. For a few people, do it by hand. For a larger cleanup, use selection and automation carefully, review the list first, and stop if Facebook shows a warning or asks you to slow down.
