The average long-time Facebook account carries hundreds of "friends," and research on real friendship (Dunbar's work put the cognitive limit around 150 relationships) says most of them are, functionally, strangers with access to your personal life. A friends-list cleanup fixes that — and done with a method, it takes one evening instead of dragging on forever. Here's the step-by-step.

## Step 1: Decide your keep rules *before* you look at the list

Scrolling your list without criteria means agonizing over every name. Decide the rules first. A good default set — keep someone if:

- You'd stop and chat if you ran into them in person, or
- They're family (even the quiet ones), or
- You've meaningfully interacted (messages, comments, real life) in the last ~2 years, or
- There's a concrete practical reason (colleague, client, community organizer).

Everyone who fails all four is a **remove**. Note what's *not* on the list: "might be awkward" isn't a keep reason — [Facebook doesn't notify people you unfriend](/blog/does-facebook-notify-unfriend), and people with 1,000 friends don't audit their count.

## Step 2: Triage the obvious removals first

Open [facebook.com/friends/list](https://www.facebook.com/friends/list) and sweep the easy categories before doing person-by-person judgment:

1. **Names you can't place.** If you need to open their profile to remember who they are, that answers it.
2. **Dead and abandoned accounts** — default photos, no activity for years.
3. **Era friends**: old jobs, old schools, old neighborhoods, where the era ended and so did the contact.
4. **Game-and-app friends** from the FarmVille years.
5. **Friend-of-friend padding** — people you accepted at 19 to be polite.

For most people, these five categories are 60–80% of the list.

## Step 3: The borderline cases

For the rest, one tiebreaker question: **"Would I share a personal life update with this person directly?"** If no — remember what friendship on Facebook actually grants: they see your friends-only posts, photos, and life events, forever, silently. That's an intimacy setting, not a politeness token. When in doubt, remove; anyone who genuinely notices and cares can send a new request, and you can accept it.

If someone truly can't be removed (boss, in-laws), use **Restrict** instead — friends on paper, but they only see your public posts.

## Step 4: Execute the removal in bulk

Here's where the plan usually dies: Facebook makes you unfriend **one person at a time**, several clicks each, with no multi-select. Three hundred removals by hand is why everyone quits halfway.
